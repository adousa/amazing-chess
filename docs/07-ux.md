## Design direction 2: *Expedition* (default stage)
A painterly, golden-hour battle stage inspired by the Belle Époque look of *Clair Obscur:
Expedition 33*. Decision: [ADR 0006](decisions/0006-expedition-battle-stage.md). Pick the stage
with the **Expedition / Seventh Seal** toggle under the board. The choice is remembered.

- **Scene:** a ruined round plaza on a headland at golden hour. A painted sky with brushy clouds,
  a low sun over the sea, floating islands, crimson trees, broken fluted columns, Art Nouveau
  lamps and petals drifting through the air. Across the sea is an island city: Haussmann-style
  buildings stacked into two hills either side of a great **iron tower whose top half is bent,
  twisted and curled over like smeared paint**. There is also a leaning lighthouse with a sweeping
  beam, and red-sailed boats. On the horizon the fractured **Monolith** stands inside a frozen
  explosion of rock, with the Stockfish Elo painted on it in gold brushstrokes. All of this is
  inspired by the game's concept art, but built in code.
- **Alive:** the headland is a meadow of about 60,000 wind-blown grass blades and 3,000
  flowers, with gusts rolling across it. The trees and bushes are CC0 models (Quaternius'
  *Stylized Nature MegaKit*) with crimson, amber and gold leaves that sway and flutter,
  on the headland and on top of the floating islands. The sea
  has Gerstner waves with whitecaps, surf breaking on the rocks, sky reflection and a glittering
  sun path. Flocks of birds wheel over the water. Render resolution scales down on slow machines.
- **Look:** an oil-paint pass (Kuwahara + brush jitter), a warm-highlight/teal-shadow grade, canvas
  weave, bloom and a vignette.
- **Cast:** our engine is **the Expedition** (ivory, gold and blue masked warriors). Stockfish is
  **the Paintress** (obsidian warriors with crimson eyes and edges). Weapons: pawn spear + shield,
  knight lance, bishop staff (casts a bolt), rook war-hammer, queen rapier, king greatsword.
- **Moves:** pawns march, knights leap, bishops glide, rooks stomp, queens dash and kings step.
  Castling moves both pieces, and promotion is a pillar of light that repaints the piece.
- **The arena is a slow elevator:** the inner circle of the plaza (inside the gold ring), with
  the board, pieces and statues on it, keeps rising out of the outer paving on a stone shaft
  with glowing gold seams. It climbs to ~16 m over about 3 minutes, eases back down and
  climbs again, shedding a trickle of dust. The camera rides it, so the board stays in the
  same place on screen while the world drops away. The
  lamps and outer ring stay on the ground. It stands still with reduced motion.
- **Captures are duels:** a camera cut over the attacker's shoulder, a letterboxed wind-up, then the
  strike in slow motion (sparks, flash, bloom, shake). A side-on close-up follows as the victim
  reels and dissolves into petals with burning edges. The attacker takes the square and the fallen
  piece stands beside the board as a small stone statue. On checkmate the king kneels and burns.
  **Duels on/off** turns the cinematics off. Autoplay waits for a duel to finish.
- **Views:** *Battle* (default, behind our army so the sea, city, tower and Monolith rise behind
  the board), *Above*, *2D*. In *Battle* the camera is free: drag to orbit around the board,
  right-drag or Shift-drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. The view is saved in the browser and
  restored on every match. **Reset camera** or a double-click brings back the default. Duel
  close-ups still take over for captures, then return to your view.
- **Subtitles** in battle style ("The Paintress's rook crushes the pawn on e4."), gilded names in
  brush-stroke banners, and title/ending cards ("The Expedition marches against The Paintress").
- **Live:** the thinking side's plinths glow, and a king in check pulses.
- **Prologue:** before the game, five beats in our own words. (1) Every year the Paintress
  paints a number on her Monolith, and no petal of Lumière may live a year beyond it. (2) Every
  Expedition that beats her makes the number climb. (3) Thanks to those who sailed before, the
  petals may now live **N** years, where N is the ladder's highest Stockfish Elo beaten (if none
  yet: "every Expedition so far has fallen"). (4) This year the Expedition sails for **this
  match's Elo**, with no names. (5) "Will this be the year Lumière wins?" About 32 s, paced for
  reading. A win's ending card says the number climbs;
  **Skip**, a click, or Esc/Enter/Space jumps straight in. Subtitles sit at the top centre
  (in the sky), clear of the pieces.
- **Soundtrack:** the *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* battle themes (a fan compilation on YouTube;
  the official "Lumière" upload, `zmvsw2ILX5k`, is the fallback if it disappears), streamed through
  YouTube's official embedded player in a *Soundtrack* section below the analysis. It starts
  with **Play** (or a live game) and pauses with **Pause**. **Music on/off** toggles it and is
  remembered. The player stays visible, because YouTube's rules don't allow hidden, audio-only
  embeds, and the audio is never downloaded.
- **Type:** Cormorant Garamond (italic display) on this stage.
- Code: `frontend/src/scene/expedition/` (world, lumiere = city/tower/Monolith, nature =
  sea/grass/trees/birds, units, fx, painterly pass, `ExpeditionScene` director). Models:
  `frontend/public/models/nature/` (see its CREDITS.md) and `frontend/src/components/ExpeditionStage.tsx`.

## Design direction 1: *The Seventh Seal*
An homage to Bergman's chess game between the Knight and Death. Decision:
[ADR 0004](decisions/0004-seventh-seal-3d-stage.md).

- **Scene:** empty stony beach, grey sea with rolling surf, overcast moving sky, a dark sea stack.
  Nothing else but the two players and the board on a flat rock. Rendered in black and white with
  film grain, flicker, vignette and the odd scratch.
- **Cast:** our engine is **the Knight** (mail shirt, seen from behind). Stockfish is **Death**
  (black hood, pale face).
- **Camera:** *Shoulder* (default) looks over the Knight's right shoulder at Death and the board.
  *Above* looks down over the board for close reading. *2D* is the flat chessground board and also
  the fallback without WebGL. The choice is remembered per browser.
- **Moves are played by hand:** one ply forward means the mover reaches, grips, lifts, carries and
  sets down the piece, leaning in when needed. Captures go beside the board on the captor's side.
  Castling moves king then rook, and promotion swaps the piece as it lands. Jumps and going back snap
  instantly. The last move's squares are faintly lit.
- **Subtitles** narrate each move in words, with the SAN and analysis badge below. A **title card**
  opens each match ("The Knight plays chess with Death"), and an **ending card** states the result.
- **Live:** the side that is thinking leans in over the board. Moves are played out as they arrive.
- **Type:** IM Fell English (display) and EB Garamond (text), self-hosted via `@fontsource`.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no move animation, flicker or camera sway.

Code: `frontend/src/scene/` (environment, figures + IK arms, pieces/board, film pass,
`SealScene` controller) and `frontend/src/components/SealStage.tsx`.

## Stack (suggestion)
React + Vite + TypeScript, **`@lichess-org/chessground`** (Lichess board: animations, arrows,
highlights, ~10 KB), `chess.js` for client-side logic, types generated from
`contracts/openapi.yaml`. Develop against the Prism mock.

## Details that make it feel great
- Move & capture sounds, check glow, last-move highlight, smooth piece animation
- Board themes (wood / marble / Lichess blue), piece sets
- Responsive; works on a projector at the demo
- Share link to a specific ply (`/replay/<id>?ply=23`)
- Result screen: "Beat Stockfish at 2200 🎉" with the replay one click away
